ART BY SAM

Website Direction & Content Structure

A clean, artistic, warm and straightforward online home for Sam’s paintings and illustration work.

1. Core Website Idea

The website should feel like stepping into Sam’s art world: personal, colourful, feminine, playful and artistic, while remaining extremely easy to understand and navigate. The design should never compete with the artwork. The paintings and illustrations are the hero.

The overall principle is: BEAUTIFUL TO LOOK AT + VERY EASY TO USE. Avoid an overly complicated portfolio, excessive text, or too many navigation layers.

3. Homepage Structure

Hero — one strong artwork or a curated rotating selection, with a simple introduction to Art by Sam.

Short artist introduction — a warm, personal paragraph and photograph/studio image.

Featured Paintings — 4–6 selected works with title and basic details.

Illustration — a clean entry point into commissioned and personal illustration work.

Selected Work / Collections — a visual grid showing the breadth of Sam’s work.

About Sam — a short story with a link to the full About page.

Contact / Commission CTA — simple invitation to enquire about a project.

Instagram or studio preview — optional, kept visually minimal.

4. PAINTINGS

Paintings should be presented as an artwork archive rather than a crowded online store. The grid should allow the images to breathe, with enough white/neutral space around each work.

Keep the contact experience very simple.

For commissions, a short enquiry form can ask: name, email, project type, date/deadline, brief description, approximate size/quantity, and budget range (optional).

10. Visual & Art Direction

The visual identity should sit between an editorial art portfolio and a charming independent artist studio.

Lots of breathing room and generous margins.

Artwork-led layouts rather than text-heavy sections.

A warm off-white or soft neutral base instead of stark digital white, if it suits the artwork photography.

A restrained but artistic accent palette pulled from Sam’s paintings.

Elegant typography paired with a subtle handwritten or human element.

Small playful details are welcome, but the interface should remain clean.

Use asymmetry sparingly for an editorial/artistic feel.

Avoid excessive animations, gradients, drop shadows, decorative icons, or trendy effects that will date quickly.

11. Typography Direction

Primary typeface: clean, modern sans-serif for navigation, descriptions and practical information.

Secondary typeface: refined serif or expressive display font for major headings and artwork titles.

Optional handwritten accent: use very sparingly for small notes, captions or personal touches.

Artwork titles can be slightly more expressive than functional website text.

12. Image Treatment

Use large, high-resolution artwork photography.

Keep colour reproduction as accurate as possible.

Mix full-bleed hero images with clean gallery grids.

Include occasional process/studio photographs to make the website feel human.

Do not over-frame every artwork; let the paintings occupy the screen.

Maintain consistent cropping rules within each gallery while allowing portrait/landscape artwork to retain its natural proportions.

14. What the Website Should Communicate Within 5 Seconds

Who is Sam?

What does she make?

Is this a professional artist?

Can I see more paintings?

Can I commission or work with her?

If a visitor cannot answer those questions quickly, the homepage is doing too much or the navigation is not clear enough.

15. The Overall Mood

Keywords: warm · feminine · artistic · colourful · personal · joyful · editorial · clean · approachable · polished

The website should feel distinctly like Sam rather than like a generic artist template. The strongest differentiator should be the combination of the artwork, the personal story, and the playful visual language—not complicated web design.
